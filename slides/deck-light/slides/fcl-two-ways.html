Before the build, the shortcut. Same four stages as the rest of the talk, sources on the left and the agent on the right. Top row: what this showcase did by hand, which is Part 3: Fivetran lands the data, and we wrote the parse, chunk, embed and classify models ourselves with the dbt_context_engineering package, into the Semantic Layer and a knowledge_base table, served over the dbt MCP server. Bottom row: Fivetran Context Layer does the middle for you, as a managed service. Its connectors bring in documents and tools like Jira, Gong, Google Drive, Confluence and Zendesk; it parses the documents, indexes them for search, and builds a context catalog of what the data means and how it relates. The result lands as plain, open tables in your own warehouse, the Agents Schema, an open-source spec, and the dbt Semantic Layer is ingested as one of its metadata sources rather than replaced. Agents read it through Agent Context MCP, or any tool with warehouse access. Be careful with the room: it is in preview, North America only at launch, and the supported destinations differ between the internal brief (Snowflake, BigQuery, Managed Data Lake Service) and the public docs (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks); check fivetran.com/docs/context-layer before naming one. And don't draw a hard line between the managed service and the dbt package: which pieces stay managed is not settled.
